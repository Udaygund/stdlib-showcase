A small statistics explainer, built with stdlib

Did my study method actually work?

You switched to flashcards (or spaced repetition, or a new app) and your quiz scores went up. Was it the method, or would scores like these turn up by luck anyway? Paste both sets of scores and find out.

2. What would luck look like?

If the method did nothing, “before” and “after” are just labels. We shuffled them times and recorded the difference in averages each time. The marker is what you actually got.

3. The textbook test

Welch’s t-test turns the same question into a single number, t, and compares it against the t-distribution. Results beyond the shaded cut-offs count as significant.

How this is computed
  • Summaries: @stdlib/stats/incr/summary
  • Welch two-sample t-test: @stdlib/stats/ttest2
  • Label shuffling (seeded, reproducible): @stdlib/random/shuffle
  • Group means for each shuffle: @stdlib/stats/strided/dmean
  • t-distribution curve and cut-offs: @stdlib/stats/base/dists/t/pdf, …/t/quantile
